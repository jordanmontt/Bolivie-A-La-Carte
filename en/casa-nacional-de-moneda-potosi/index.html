Casa Nacional de Moneda (Potosí)

Potosí Department · Culture and history

Built between 1753 and 1773 to mint the silver of the Cerro Rico, the Potosí mint is one of the largest civil buildings of colonial America. Its thick walls still hold the wooden rolling mills once turned by mules, the smelting furnaces and three centuries of coins. There is colonial painting too (including the famous Virgin of the Cerro) and, in the courtyard, the mascarón, a large smiling face that became the symbol of the city.

High altitude. Above 3,500 m, altitude sickness (soroche) is common. Arrive gradually, drink plenty and take it slow on the first day.

Things to do

  • Guided tour of the minting rooms and the wooden rolling mills
  • The Virgin of the Cerro and the Potosí school of painting
  • The mascarón in the inner courtyard

What to eat

  • KalapurkaPotosí's spicy corn soup, served bubbling thanks to a volcanic stone heated in the fire.
  • SalteñaBolivia's pastry: slightly sweet dough with a juicy filling of meat, potato and olives. Eaten in the morning, without spilling the juice!
Tip. Guided tours only (about 2 h, in Spanish or English depending on the time), usually closed on Mondays. The rooms are cold, so bring a jacket.

How to get to Casa de Moneda

  • Potosí Road (bus, car) · 5 min on foot

    One block from the main square (10 de Noviembre).
