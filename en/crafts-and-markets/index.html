Culture and traditions

Crafts and markets

Weaving, carved wood, silver, and the markets where everything is sold.

FestivalsFoodUNESCO heritageCrafts and marketsMyths and legends

Yampara weaving from Tarabuco

Finely patterned cloth, often showing everyday life and animals, hand-woven on the loom. The Sunday market is the best place to find it.

Where: Tarabuco · Chuquisaca Candelaria · Chuquisaca

Jalq'a textiles

Red and black, full of fantastic creatures from the underworld: the weavings of the Jalq'a communities around Maragua are among the most striking in the Andes.

Where: Maragua Crater · Chuquisaca

Kallawaya weaving

Around Charazani, weaving goes hand in hand with traditional medicine: coca-leaf bags and brightly coloured ponchos, made in the communities.

Where: Charazani and Apolobamba · La Paz

Chiquitos woodcarving

Angels, saints and altarpieces: San Miguel's workshops carry on the carving of the Jesuit missions, and still restore the region's churches.

Where: Missions: San Miguel de Velasco · Santa Cruz

Aiquile charangos

The small ten-string Andean guitar, made by Aiquile's luthiers. You can order one made to measure.

Where: Aiquile · Cochabamba

Totora reed boats

On Lake Titicaca, boats are still built from the lake's reed. Huatajata's craftsmen also make small models.

Where: Huatajata · La Paz

Potosí silver

Silver jewellery and small objects, in the town that minted the Spanish empire's coins. Buy in shops, with a hallmark.

Where: Potosí

Alpaca and llama wool

Warm jumpers, scarves and ponchos for the Altiplano. In La Paz, Sagárnaga and Linares streets are full of them; check that it really is wool.

Where: La Paz

Tiwanaku ceramics and stone

In the village, craftsmen reproduce Tiwanaku ceramics and sculptures. Fine copies, not to be confused with ancient pieces.

Where: Tiwanaku · La Paz

Feria 16 de Julio

On Thursdays and Sundays, miles of stalls in El Alto selling everything from car parts to cholita clothes. Visit without anything valuable.

Where: El Alto · La Paz

Witches' Market

Linares and Jiménez streets in La Paz: herbs, amulets, llama foetuses and offerings for Pachamama, prepared by the yatiris.

Where: La Paz

La Cancha

One of the largest open-air markets in South America, in Cochabamba: fruit, spices, crafts, everything is sold in a maze of streets.

Where: Cochabamba

Los Pozos market

Santa Cruz's market for tropical fruit, fresh juices and local food: ideal for trying a majadito or a cuñapé.

Where: Santa Cruz de la Sierra · Santa Cruz

Cotoca pottery

Jugs, pots and terracotta figures made by Cotoca's potters, right next to Santa Cruz. You'll find them around the sanctuary.

Where: Cotoca · Santa Cruz
