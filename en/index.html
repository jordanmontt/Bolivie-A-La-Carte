La Paz, the Cordillera and the Yungas

The world's highest city, the glaciers of the Cordillera Real and the drop into the Yungas forests.

  • La PazThe world's highest seat of government, in a bowl at the foot of Illimani.
  • El AltoThe big Aymara city on the Altiplano, right above La Paz: a giant market, cholets and cholita wrestling.
  • Valle de la LunaMoon-like clay formations in the southern suburbs of La Paz.
  • Valle de las ÁnimasA forest of earth spires on the edge of La Paz, bigger and wilder than the Valley of the Moon.
  • ChacaltayaA former ski area at 5,400 m: the highest view of the Cordillera Real.
  • Huayna PotosíA 6,000 m peak known as one of the most accessible, with a guided 2 or 3-day climb.
  • Cordillera Real: CondoririGlacial lagoons and snowy peaks, two hours from La Paz.
  • TiwanakuUNESCO-listed pre-Inca site: the Gate of the Sun and the Kalasasaya temple.
  • Uru community of IruitoAn Uru community on the Desaguadero river, keepers of a very old lake culture.
  • Jesús de MachacaAn Aymara Altiplano village and its 1706 church, south of Lake Titicaca.
  • Comanche and its Puya raimondiiA forest of Puya raimondii, the world's largest bromeliad, on the Altiplano.
  • Viscachani hot springsWarm-water baths on the Altiplano, on the road to Oruro, about 1 h 30 from La Paz.
  • SorataA quiet village at the foot of Illampu, a base for treks.
  • Charazani and ApolobambaLand of the Kallawaya Andean healers (UNESCO), with hot springs and great treks.
  • Death Road (bike)The world's most famous mountain bike descent: from 4,650 m to 1,200 m in a day.
  • Choro trek (Inca trail)Three days on a pre-Hispanic trail, from the snows of La Cumbre down to the Yungas forest.
  • Takesi trek (Inca trail)Two days on one of Bolivia's best-preserved Inca trails, down to Yanacachi.
  • Yunga Cruz trekThe wildest pre-Hispanic trail down to the Yungas: 4 to 5 days below Illimani.
  • Coroico (Yungas)A hilltop village in the subtropical Yungas: coffee, waterfalls and pools with a view.
  • Chulumani (South Yungas)A small South Yungas town among coffee, coca and cloud forest, away from the crowds.

Lake Titicaca

The sacred lake of the Incas: Copacabana, Isla del Sol and Isla de la Luna, at 3,800 m.

  • Copacabana (Lake Titicaca)A small town on the shore of Lake Titicaca, gateway to the Isla del Sol.
  • Isla del SolSacred island of the Incas: trails, ruins and views of the Cordillera Real.
  • Isla de la LunaA small neighbouring island with the temple of the Virgins of the Sun (Iñak Uyu).
  • HuatajataA Lake Titicaca village, home of the totora reed boats.
  • Suriki IslandThe island of reed-boat builders, including those of the Ra II expedition.
  • Isla ParitiA small Titicaca island and its museum of Tiwanaku pottery found on the spot.

Uyuni Salt Flat and the Altiplano

The world's largest salt flat, red lagoons, Sajama volcano and the canyons of Tupiza.

  • Sajama National ParkBolivia's highest peak (6,542 m), geysers, hot springs and llamas.
  • Curahuara de CarangasThe “Sistine Chapel of the Altiplano”: an adobe church covered in murals.
  • OruroAn Altiplano mining city, famous for its UNESCO-listed carnival. Starting point of the train to Uyuni.
  • Obrajes and Capachos hot springsNatural hot-water pools on the Altiplano, less than an hour from Oruro.
  • Machacamarca railway museumOld steam locomotives and the history of the mining railways, south of Oruro.
  • Lakes Uru Uru and PoopóShallow Altiplano lakes on Oruro's doorstep, visited by flamingos.
  • ChipayaVillage of the Uru-Chipaya, one of the oldest peoples of the Andes, with round earthen houses.
  • Coipasa Salt FlatBolivia's second-largest salt flat, wild and almost without visitors.
  • UyuniBase town for Salar tours, with a train cemetery on the edge of town.
  • Salar de Uyuni + Sud Lípez (tour)The world's largest salt flat, then the colourful lagoons of the Sud Lípez. The highlight of many trips.
  • TupizaWild West scenery: red canyons, cacti and the last ride of Butch Cassidy.
  • CotagaitaA small green town in a fruit-growing valley, on the road from Potosí to Villazón.
  • VillazónThe border with Argentina, facing La Quiaca: the crossing travellers use most.

Sucre and Potosí

The white city of Sucre, Potosí's silver mines and the weavers of Tarabuco.

  • PotosíOnce the richest city in the world thanks to the silver of Cerro Rico. UNESCO-listed.
  • Casa Nacional de Moneda (Potosí)The royal mint where Cerro Rico silver became coins, now Bolivia's great museum.
  • Ojo del Inca (Tarapaya)A perfectly round warm-water lagoon, 30 minutes from Potosí.
  • SucreThe “white city” and constitutional capital: the most beautiful colonial city in the country.
  • TarabucoBig Sunday market of the Yampara communities, with remarkable textiles.
  • CandelariaA Yampara village beyond Tarabuco: an old hacienda, weaving and rural tourism.
  • ZudáñezA small valley town linked to the wars of independence, east of Sucre.
  • Maragua CraterA giant syncline in reds and purples, with Jalq'a weaving villages.

Tarija, wine country and the Chaco

The mild, sunny south: high-altitude vineyards, singani, the Tariquía forests and the heat of the Gran Chaco.

  • TarijaBolivia's Andalusia: high-altitude vineyards, singani and a relaxed vibe.
  • Valle de la ConcepciónTarija's wine and singani cellars.
  • San LorenzoA colonial Chapaco village, home of the hero Moto Méndez, 15 km from Tarija.
  • CoimataA river, natural pools and a tall waterfall, 20 minutes from Tarija.
  • San Jacinto lake and TomatitasA lake among the hills and a river for swimming, on Tarija's doorstep.
  • Padcaya and ChaguayaA valley village south of Tarija, and the great sanctuary of the Virgin of Chaguaya.
  • BermejoA tropical border town on the Bermejo river, facing Argentina.
  • Sama reserve: Tajzara lagoonsHigh lagoons, dunes and flamingos in the Sama reserve, above Tarija.
  • Tariquía reserveCloud forests and wild rivers, for multi-day treks with a guide.
  • Camargo (Cinti valley)Old vineyards and red cliffs in the Cinti valley, between Potosí and Tarija.
  • Villamontes (Gran Chaco)Gateway to the Gran Chaco, on the Pilcomayo river and its canyon, El Angosto.
  • YacuibaThe Chaco's big border town, facing Argentina.
  • CamiriThe Chaco's "oil capital", among hills and Guaraní culture.

Cochabamba and its valleys

The country's breadbasket: markets, chicha, Inca ruins and the dinosaur tracks of Torotoro.

  • CochabambaThe “city of eternal spring” and the food capital of the country.
  • Laguna AlalayA large lagoon in the heart of Cochabamba, a bird refuge below the hills.
  • Pairumani and Villa AlbinaThe villa of tin baron Simón Patiño, its gardens, and the start of a trail to the Tunari.
  • Tunari National ParkThe mountains right above the city: high lagoons and the Tunari summit (5,035 m).
  • Apote and its twin waterfallsA mountain valley above Tiquipaya, with two waterfalls side by side and trails towards the Tunari.
  • YungapampaA refuge above a sea of clouds, on the north side of the Tunari, Andean bear country.
  • Laguna CoraniA large reservoir among pine forests and mountains, known for its trout.
  • IncachacaA cloud forest at the edge of the Chapare, with an Inca bridge, waterfalls and an old power plant.
  • Valle Alto: Tarata and ClizaColonial valley villages, markets and chicha.
  • Quillacollo and Inca RakayInca ruins perched above Sipe Sipe, and Quillacollo, home of the Urkupiña festival.
  • Torotoro National ParkDinosaur footprints, a spectacular canyon and the Umajalanta cave.
  • IncallajtaThe largest Inca site in Bolivia, in a remote valley.
  • TotoraA small, well-preserved colonial village on the old road to Santa Cruz.
  • AiquileBolivia's national charango capital, halfway between Cochabamba and Sucre.
  • MizqueA colonial village in a warm valley, home of the red-fronted macaw.

Santa Cruz, Samaipata and Amboró

Tropical lowlands, the village of Samaipata and the cloud forests of Amboró.

  • Santa Cruz de la SierraThe big, modern tropical city of the east, with an international airport (Viru Viru).
  • CotocaThe sanctuary of the Virgin of Cotoca and its potters, 20 km from Santa Cruz.
  • Lomas de ArenaWhite sand dunes and lagoons south of Santa Cruz.
  • EspejillosNatural pools and waterfalls on a river near Santa Cruz.
  • Laguna VolcánA lake hidden in a bowl of forested mountains.
  • SamaipataA bohemian village between the Andes and the forest: El Fuerte (UNESCO) and Amboró park.
  • Amboró National ParkCloud forests and tree ferns, very rich in birds.
  • Buena Vista (northern Amboró)A small village at the foot of Amboró park, on the rainforest side: rivers, birds and coffee.
  • VallegrandeA small colonial mountain town at the heart of the Che Route.
  • La HigueraThe hamlet where Che was executed in 1967, now a place of memory.

Chiquitos Jesuit Missions

Painted wooden Jesuit missions, still in use, and further north Noel Kempff Mercado park.

  • Missions: San JavierThe first of the Jesuit missions of Chiquitos (UNESCO), with painted wooden churches.
  • Missions: ConcepciónThe most beautiful square of the missions, with a restoration workshop and a museum.
  • Missions: San Ignacio de VelascoThe hub of the missions circuit, with its lagoon.
  • Missions: San Miguel de VelascoA Chiquitano mission with woodcarving workshops that are still very much alive.
  • Missions: Santa Ana de VelascoThe most authentic of the Chiquitos missions, with its original organ.
  • Missions: San Rafael de VelascoA mission church decorated with mica and painted wood, between San Ignacio and San José.
  • Missions: San José de ChiquitosThe only stone Jesuit mission, at the foot of the hills.
  • Noel Kempff Mercado National ParkA vast and very remote UNESCO park: the Huanchaca plateau and the Arco Iris falls.

Roboré and the Chiquitos hills

Red cliffs, waterfalls, a river of hot water and the 'death train' towards Brazil.

  • Chochís and the Torre de DavidA huge red cliff, the Torre de David, with a sanctuary at its foot and waterfalls in the Tucavaca valley.
  • RoboréA quiet small town in the Chiquitanía, a base for Santiago de Chiquitos, Chochís and Aguas Calientes.
  • Santiago de ChiquitosAn old mission village up in the hills: viewpoints, hikes, caves and waterfalls.
  • Aguas Calientes (hot river)A natural hot-water river (around 38 °C) in the middle of the forest, where you swim among small fish.

Amazon

Madidi jungle and the Yacuma pampas: monkeys, caimans and pink dolphins.

  • RurrenabaqueGateway to the Bolivian Amazon, on the Beni river.
  • Madidi National ParkOne of the most biodiverse places on the planet. Primary forest.
  • Yacuma PampasWetlands teeming with animals: caimans, capybaras and pink river dolphins.
  • Barba Azul reserveBeni savannas, refuge of the blue-throated macaw, one of the rarest parrots in the world.
  • Beni Biosphere ReserveSavannas, forests and lagoons near San Borja: a reserve rich in wildlife, still rarely visited.
  • Trinidad (Llanos de Moxos)Capital of the Beni, with flood plains and Moxeño culture.
  • Laguna SuárezA big, quiet lagoon on Trinidad's doorstep, for swimming and eating fish.
  • San Ignacio de MoxosA former Jesuit mission on the Beni plains, famous for the Ichapekene Piesta (UNESCO).
  • CobijaThe capital of Pando, on the Acre river facing Brazil: gateway to the northern Amazon.
  • Porvenir and the Tahuamanu riverAn Amazon village near Cobija: boat trips on the Tahuamanu and a natural pool.
  • Puerto Rico and the Manuripi reserveA small town where two rivers meet, gateway to the Manuripi Amazon reserve.
  • RiberaltaAn Amazon river port where the Beni meets the Madre de Dios, capital of the Brazil nut.
  • GuayaramerínA border town on the Mamoré river, facing the Brazilian town of Guajará-Mirim.
