La Paz

La Paz Department · Cities

La Paz is like nowhere else: set in a bowl at 3,600 m below the snowy peak of Illimani, it's best seen from cable cars gliding over the rooftops. You'll pass cholitas in bowler hats and witches selling dried llama foetuses for good luck, between colonial churches and markets spilling onto the pavements. Fascinating, chaotic and breathtaking, in every sense.

High altitude. Above 3,500 m, altitude sickness (soroche) is common. Arrive gradually, drink plenty and take it slow on the first day.

Things to do

  • Mi Teleférico, the urban cable car network
  • Witches' Market and Calle Jaén
  • Plaza Murillo and San Francisco church
  • Rodríguez market and the flavours of La Paz
  • Killi Killi viewpoint at sunset

What to eat

  • La Paz salteñasIn La Paz, people queue in the morning at famous salteñerías. Have one with a fruit juice.
  • AnticuchosMarinated beef heart skewers grilled on the street at night, with potato and a spicy peanut sauce.
  • Plato paceñoA typical La Paz plate: corn, broad beans, potatoes and fried cheese.
  • Api con pastelA thick hot drink of purple corn and cinnamon, with a cheese pastry dusted with sugar. The market breakfast.
Tip. Arriving by road from a lower city (Cochabamba, Sucre) helps you acclimatise. Take the first day slowly, with coca tea.

Also here

  • Festivals · UNESCO AlasitasThe miniatures fair: people buy small versions of what they wish for (a house, a car, banknotes, a diploma) and have them blessed for Ekeko, the god of abundance.
  • Festivals · UNESCO Gran PoderLa Paz's great folk parade: thousands of morenada, caporales and diablada dancers cross the city in gold-embroidered costumes.
  • Festivals Ñatitas festivalAt La Paz's general cemetery, skulls decorated with flowers and sunglasses are honoured and thanked for their protection. A striking tradition, to be respected.
  • Crafts and markets Alpaca and llama woolWarm jumpers, scarves and ponchos for the Altiplano. In La Paz, Sagárnaga and Linares streets are full of them; check that it really is wool.
  • Crafts and markets Witches' MarketLinares and Jiménez streets in La Paz: herbs, amulets, llama foetuses and offerings for Pachamama, prepared by the yatiris.
  • Myths and legends EkekoA small moustached man loaded with bags of food, banknotes and miniature objects, Ekeko is the Aymara god of abundance. People offer him a cigarette and trust him with their wishes, especially during Alasitas.
  • Myths and legends Illimani and MururataMountains are achachilas, protective ancestors. It is said that Illimani, in a fit of jealousy, knocked off Mururata's head with a sling: that's why its neighbour has a flat top.

How to get to La Paz

  • El Alto Road (bus, car) · 15 to 30 min

    Mi Teleférico (red, yellow or blue lines from La Paz) or minibus.

    The cable car is the easiest way, with a superb view over the city.

  • Valle de la Luna Road (bus, car) · 30 to 45 min

    Minibus from the centre, or taxi.

  • Cochabamba Flight · 45 min flight

    Daily BoA flights (several a day) to El Alto airport.

    You land straight at 4,000 m: worse for acclimatisation.

  • Rurrenabaque Flight · 40 to 50 min flight

    BoA and EcoJet flights (small planes, often full: book ahead).

    A spectacular flight over the Andes.

  • Sucre Flight · 55 min flight

    BoA flights.

  • Death Road Bike · Full day

    Mountain bike tour with an agency (transport, bike, meals). Bus back to La Paz in the evening, or stay in Coroico.

  • Choro trek Road (bus, car) · 1 h

    Minibus or taxi to La Cumbre, start of the trek.

  • Trinidad Flight · 1 h flight

    BoA flights.

  • Uyuni Flight · 1 h flight

    BoA flights almost every day.

    Saves you a night on the bus.

  • Tarija Flight · 1 h flight

    BoA flights.

  • Santa Cruz Flight · 1 h flight

    Very frequent BoA flights.

  • Cobija Flight · 1 h 05 flight

    BoA flights almost daily from El Alto airport.

  • Chacaltaya Road (bus, car) · 1 h 30

    Minibus tour from La Paz (often combined with Valle de la Luna).

  • Huayna Potosí Road (bus, car) · 1 h 30

    Transport included in the guided climb.

  • Tiwanaku Road (bus, car) · 1 h 30

    Minibus from the La Paz cemetery area, or a tour.

  • Huatajata Road (bus, car) · 1 h 30

    La Paz – Copacabana bus (get off at Huatajata).

  • Riberalta Flight · 1 h 30 flight

    BoA flights, not every day.

  • Viscachani Road (bus, car) · about 1 h 30

    Minibus from El Alto towards Patacamaya (Viscachani stop).

  • Condoriri Road (bus, car) · 2 h

    Private vehicle or agency.

  • Takesi trek Road (bus, car) · 2 h 30

    Minibus to Ventilla, then up to the start of the trek.

  • Comanche Road (bus, car) · 2 to 3 h

    Car with driver or tour from La Paz.

  • Coroico Road (bus, car) · 3 h

    Minibus from the Minasa terminal (Villa Fátima).

  • Jesús de Machaca Road (bus, car) · about 3 h

    Bus from El Alto via Viacha.

  • Yunga Cruz Road (bus, car) · about 3 h

    Transport to the start of the trek, arranged by the agency.

  • Copacabana Road (bus, car) · 3 h 30 to 4 h

    Tourist buses and minibuses from the cemetery area (Av. Baptista) or through an agency.

    Keep your documents with you: you get off the bus at Tiquina.

  • Sorata Road (bus, car) · 3 h 30

    Minibus from the cemetery area.

  • Oruro Road (bus, car) · 3 h 30 to 4 h

    Buses every 30 minutes.

  • Chulumani Road (bus, car) · about 4 h

    Minibus from Villa Fátima, in La Paz.

  • Curahuara Road (bus, car) · about 4 h

    Bus towards Tambo Quemado / Arica: get off at the turn-off, then take a taxi.

    Combine with Sajama park.

  • Sajama Road (bus, car) · 4 h 30

    Bus towards Arica (Chile), get off at the Sajama junction, then a taxi. Easier by car or on a tour.

  • Cochabamba Road (bus, car) · 7 to 8 h

    Day or night buses from the Cochabamba bus terminal (many companies, every hour). A “cama” (sleeper) bus is best at night.

    A beautiful climb up to the Altiplano. Travel by day to enjoy the views.

  • Charazani Road (bus, car) · 8 to 10 h

    Bus from La Paz's cemetery district, few departures a week.

    Long road, partly unpaved.

  • Potosí Road (bus, car) · 8 to 10 h (overnight)

    Night bus.

  • Uyuni Road (bus, car) · 9 to 11 h (overnight)

    Tourist night buses (Todo Turismo, Trans Omar…) from the La Paz bus terminal.

  • Rurrenabaque Road (bus, car) · 12 to 15 h

    Night bus from the Minasa terminal (Villa Fátima).

More places in this region

  • El AltoThe big Aymara city on the Altiplano, right above La Paz: a giant market, cholets and cholita wrestling.
  • Valle de la LunaMoon-like clay formations in the southern suburbs of La Paz.
  • ChacaltayaA former ski area at 5,400 m: the highest view of the Cordillera Real.
  • Huayna PotosíA 6,000 m peak known as one of the most accessible, with a guided 2 or 3-day climb.
  • Cordillera Real: CondoririGlacial lagoons and snowy peaks, two hours from La Paz.
  • TiwanakuUNESCO-listed pre-Inca site: the Gate of the Sun and the Kalasasaya temple.
  • Uru community of IruitoAn Uru community on the Desaguadero river, keepers of a very old lake culture.
  • Jesús de MachacaAn Aymara Altiplano village and its 1706 church, south of Lake Titicaca.
  • Comanche and its Puya raimondiiA forest of Puya raimondii, the world's largest bromeliad, on the Altiplano.
  • Viscachani hot springsWarm-water baths on the Altiplano, on the road to Oruro, about 1 h 30 from La Paz.
  • SorataA quiet village at the foot of Illampu, a base for treks.
  • Charazani and ApolobambaLand of the Kallawaya Andean healers (UNESCO), with hot springs and great treks.
  • Death Road (bike)The world's most famous mountain bike descent: from 4,650 m to 1,200 m in a day.
  • Choro trek (Inca trail)Three days on a pre-Hispanic trail, from the snows of La Cumbre down to the Yungas forest.
  • Takesi trek (Inca trail)Two days on one of Bolivia's best-preserved Inca trails, down to Yanacachi.
  • Yunga Cruz trekThe wildest pre-Hispanic trail down to the Yungas: 4 to 5 days below Illimani.
  • Coroico (Yungas)A hilltop village in the subtropical Yungas: coffee, waterfalls and pools with a view.
  • Chulumani (South Yungas)A small South Yungas town among coffee, coca and cloud forest, away from the crowds.
